How to use ReadyWise.

What to do, what ReadyWise asks for and why, why research comes first, and what happens to your data. It takes about ten minutes to read, and five to get started.

Start in five minutes

  1. Create an account.

    Sign-up takes you into a short guided set-up, one question at a time. Skip anything you like. You can fill it in later from Profile and CV.

  2. Add your CV.

    Optional, but it changes the interview most. Drop in a PDF or Word file, or paste the text. ReadyWise reads it into a structured profile.

  3. Start a new interview.

    Choose what you are preparing for: a job, internship, graduate scheme, university place, scholarship, visa, fellowship, investor pitch or certification. Add the company or institution, the role, the date and the description if you have one.

  4. Run the research.

    ReadyWise looks into your specific opportunity. It takes a minute or two, and you can watch each step happen. Every finding says how sure it is.

  5. Meet your panel.

    Up to three interviewers and a difficulty are assigned from what the research found. You do not pick them, just like a real process.

  6. Follow your plan.

    You get a day-by-day plan. Tick tasks off as you finish them. It changes if you struggle with something or miss a day.

  7. Practise in the room.

    Start a session, allow the microphone (and the camera if you want), and answer out loud or by typing. The panel follows up, challenges you and watches the clock.

  8. Read your report, then go again.

    Replay any moment by timestamp, see what to fix first, and what to ask the real interviewer. Each session updates your plan.

What we ask, and why

ReadyWise is not a list of questions. It builds your interview from what you tell it, and every answer changes something you can see. Nothing is mandatory. If you leave something out, that part falls back to general preparation.

The rule of thumb: the more specific you are, the more specific your preparation. A real job description beats a job title, and a CV beats no CV.

Why research comes first

Practising for the wrong interview is worse than not practising. Formats, panels and questions differ between companies, programmes and countries, and most of that is written down somewhere, if you know where to look. ReadyWise looks first, so every session after that is aimed at the real thing.

What it finds

  • The stages and format of the process, and how long it takes.
  • Questions that candidates report being asked.
  • What impressed interviewers and what hurt people.
  • The skills the role or programme really demands.
  • Official requirements and timing, from the source.

Where it looks, and how sure it is

ReadyWise reads official pages, candidate accounts on forums and review sites, and interview videos with their transcripts. Each finding carries a label, and the sources are linked so you can check them.

confirmed

Backed by an official source.

candidate-reported

From people who went through it. A strong lead, never treated as fact.

inferred

A reasonable expectation from the role. Nothing external behind it yet.

Why it happens before everything else

The research decides who sits on your panel, how hard they push, and what goes into your plan. That is why the panel is assigned after research and not chosen by you.

When to run it again

Refresh the research when you get a new job description, when the date changes, or about a week before the interview. Processes change, and newer accounts matter.

Know the limits. Search can miss things, and candidate accounts can be wrong or out of date. Use candidate-reported findings to prepare, and always check the official page for dates, fees and requirements.

How feedback and scores work

  • Rubrics, not vibes. Each skill is scored against a rubric, using what you actually said in the session.
  • Timestamps. Every note points to a moment you can replay, with a stronger version of weak answers.
  • Skills memory. Scores are blended over time, so one bad day does not define you and one good day does not hide a gap.
  • Each interviewer sees only what they need. The technical lead hears about your technical gaps, the HR director about your behavioural ones.
  • Practice, not prediction. Scores describe how you performed in practice. They never predict whether you will be hired, admitted or granted a visa.

Your data and your control

What is kept

Your profile and CV text, your interview set-ups, short research notes with links to their sources (never full web pages or transcripts), and your session turns, scores and notes. This is what makes your plan, your replays and your skills memory work.

Who processes it

Third-party AI services (currently Groq) do the research, run the interviewers, speak their lines and write the feedback. The text you provide and the answers you give are sent to them for that job.

Microphone and camera

They start only after you begin a session and allow the browser prompt. The camera is optional, and you can always type instead of speaking.

What you control

  • Skip any question, and edit your profile and CV whenever you like.
  • Delete an interview workspace whenever you want. It is removed from your account.
  • Choose how much to share. Less data means more general preparation, never a blocked account.

Questions about your data? Send us a message and we will answer by email.

Getting the most from it

  • Paste the real job or programme description, not just a title.
  • Add your CV before your first session.
  • Do the daily tasks. They are short, and the plan adapts to what you finish.
  • Practise in the same format as the real thing: same length, same setting, same language.
  • Replay the “improve” moments before you book the next session.
  • Raise the pressure only when standard sessions feel comfortable.
  • Use headphones in a quiet room so the panel hears you clearly.
  • Refresh the research a week before the date.

If something goes wrong

The microphone or voice recognition does not work

Use Chrome or Edge for voice recognition. The camera and microphone need a secure connection (HTTPS, or localhost when running locally) and your permission in the browser. Typing your answers works everywhere.

Research is taking a long time

Each angle is searched and checked separately, so a minute or two is normal. If it stops, close the window and run the research again.

I skipped the set-up questions

That is fine. Open Profile and CV at any time to add your details.

The date of my interview changed

Update the date in your interview workspace, then refresh the research and your plan so the days and priorities match.
